\chapter*{Abstract}
\cftaddtitleline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}{\thepage}

\emph{Corrosion is a persistent threat to steel structures in the oil and
gas, marine and civil sectors, and whether a corroded component remains fit
for service depends on how deep the metal loss goes as well as how much area
it covers. Camera-based deep learning has made contactless corrosion
detection practical, but a color camera records appearance only: it can
confuse stains, coatings and shadows with corrosion, it cannot see beneath
intact paint, and it reports severity as affected area rather than loss of
material. Thermal imaging responds to defects below the surface and LiDAR
measures surface geometry, yet of the 33 corrosion-sensing studies reviewed,
16 rely on RGB images alone, only one adds thermal imaging, and none fuses
RGB, thermal and LiDAR data. The aim of this
study is to develop and evaluate a machine learning framework that detects
corroded regions on metal surfaces by fusing RGB, thermal and LiDAR data,
measures the corroded area and metal loss of each region from the LiDAR, and
determines what each sensor contributes to detection. The three sensors,
carried on an unmanned aerial vehicle, will be registered to a common
reference frame, and the registered images stacked into one five-channel
input to a pretrained YOLO11 detector. An ablation will then compare each
sensor alone, the paired configurations and the full combination, with a
blank-channel control, under identical conditions and a decision rule fixed
in advance. A
preliminary test on MID-1K, a public RGB--thermal--LiDAR person-detection
dataset, ran end to end across six input configurations and three random
seeds. Early fusion reached a mean test mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.324 against 0.311
for RGB alone, a gain smaller than the spread between seeds and so short of
the decision rule, while the same thermal images fell from 0.756 to 0.521
mAP@0.5 when scored against RGB-aligned labels, showing that pixel-level
registration must precede fusion. The expected contributions are a registered RGB--thermal--LiDAR
corrosion dataset and a controlled measure of which sensors are worth
deploying for corrosion detection.}

\vspace{0.8\baselineskip}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} \emph{Corrosion detection; multimodal sensor
fusion; early fusion; object detection; YOLO; thermal imaging; LiDAR; ablation
study.}
